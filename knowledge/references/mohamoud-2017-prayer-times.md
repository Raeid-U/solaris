## Scope

This paper applies spherical trigonometry to Islamic prayer-time calculation, with case studies for East African capital cities and Mecca.

## Extracted Takeaways

- The paper models prayer times from solar position, declination, elevation/depression angles, equation of time, hour angle, and shadow length.
- It emphasizes Asr shadow modelling and zenith/noon shadow length.
- It adopts an `18°` solar depression angle for both Fajr and Isha in its calculations, while acknowledging that different institutions use different angles.
- It lists institutional Fajr/Isha angle conventions, including Muslim World League, ISNA, Egyptian survey authority, Umm al-Qura, Karachi, Tehran, and Shia Ithna-Ashari/Qum entries.
- It uses `-0.833°` for actual sunrise/sunset, associating the correction with refraction and solar semidiameter.
- It describes the Hanafi Asr case as using twice the object height plus the zenith shadow, while describing the majority case as one object height plus the zenith shadow.
- It contains religious-source references and fiqh claims, but Solaris should not treat those as independently verified without scholar review.
